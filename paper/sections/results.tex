\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}

Numbers come first in each subsection and interpretation is separated from
them. Every macro-F1 is reported against its own pair's chance floor, and
because that floor is pair-dependent, nothing is averaged across pairs. Unless
stated otherwise, intervals are 95\% paired cluster bootstrap intervals over
target-test speakers and seeds.

The complete set of numbers, each with its interval, its floor and the run
filter that produced it, is released with the code as a single generated
document; every figure and table here is produced from the result files by
script.

\label{sec:results-headline}

Table~\ref{tab:headline} gives the configuration selected on source-side
validation alone, alongside the best configuration present in the grid.

Validated macro-F1 is $0.3156$ $[0.1880, 0.4432]$ for
RAVDESS $\rightarrow$ CREMA-D against a chance floor of $0.1665$, and
$0.4994$ $[0.4592, 0.5395]$ for CREMA-D $\rightarrow$ RAVDESS against

\subsection{The alignment ladder}
\label{sec:results-ladder}

Table~\ref{tab:ladder} gives target macro-F1 by rung with both discrepancy
columns, and Figure~\ref{fig:ladder} plots them together.

    and the chance floor) against marginal discrepancy in the rung's own

    \texttt{none} and is then flat, while discrepancy continues to fall by two

Moving from \texttt{none} to any aligned rung is worth between $+0.1208$ and
$+0.1359$ for RAVDESS $\rightarrow$ CREMA-D and between $+0.1524$ and
$+0.1961$ for CREMA-D $\rightarrow$ RAVDESS. All ten of these contrasts, and
both aggregation contrasts, are in the pre-registered primary family
(Table~\ref{tab:primary}); \textbf{all 14 survive Holm correction}, with
adjusted $p$ at the bootstrap's resolution floor.

\input{../tables/primary}

Among the five aligned rungs, the largest difference is $0.0151$ in the forward
direction and $0.0437$ in the reverse --- an order of magnitude smaller than the
step off \texttt{none}. Those contrasts are outside the pre-registered family
and we make no significance claim about them; the interval is reported and
nothing more is asserted.

The ordering does not track discrepancy. \texttt{mkmmd\_full} reaches the lowest
marginal discrepancy in \emph{both} geometries --- $12.04$ and $23.25$ times the
same-distribution null in the forward direction, against \texttt{zscore}'s
$51.79$ and $480.85$ --- and is never the best rung. In the reverse direction it
is the \emph{worst} aligned rung, at $0.3885$ $[0.3653, 0.4099]$ against
\texttt{zscore}'s $0.4321$ $[0.4070, 0.4547]$.

\paragraph{What this does and does not show.} Alignment helps, substantially and
reproducibly. But the entire benefit is available from per-dimension
standardisation, and matching further moments --- covariance in the CORAL case,
higher-order structure in the MK-MMD case --- buys nothing measurable while
reducing marginal discrepancy by one to two orders of magnitude. That
combination is what the rest of this section explains rather than merely
reports.

\paragraph{A caveat that must accompany the MK-MMD rows.} In
\textbf{64.9\%} of \texttt{mkmmd\_diag} runs and \textbf{55.2\%} of
\texttt{mkmmd\_full} runs, optimisation failed to improve on its CORAL warm
start and the fitted map reverted to it. In those cells the rung is CORAL, and
reporting it as a distinct sixth rung without saying so would overstate what was
fitted.

\subsection{Mechanism: alignment removes the marginal term, not the conditional one}
\label{sec:decomposition}

Shift between two corpora decomposes into three parts that different methods
address: label shift ($P(y)$ differs), covariate shift ($P(x)$ differs), and
conditional shift ($P(x \mid y)$ differs). Feature alignment addresses only the
second. Table~\ref{tab:decomposition} measures all three.

\input{../tables/decomposition}

\begin{figure}[tb]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{decomposition.pdf}
  \caption{Marginal and class-conditional discrepancy across the ladder (left
    axis, log scale) with their ratio (right axis). Alignment drives the
    marginal term down far faster than the conditional term, so the residual
    after alignment is almost entirely conditional.}
  \label{fig:decomposition}
\end{figure}

\textbf{Label shift is negligible.} The KL divergence between the realised
partition priors is $0.02235$ nats for RAVDESS $\rightarrow$ CREMA-D and
$0.02541$ nats for the reverse, with total variation $0.085$. As
Section~\ref{sec:corpora} noted, the entire difference sits in one class and is
an artefact of merging RAVDESS \texttt{calm} into \texttt{neutral}.

\textbf{Alignment removes the marginal term far faster than the conditional
one.} Taking \texttt{none} as the baseline and \texttt{mkmmd\_full} as the
endpoint, forward, marginal discrepancy falls from $1496.1$ to $15.3$ --- a
factor of $0.010$ --- while conditional discrepancy falls only from $215.1$ to
$14.0$, a factor of $0.07$. The consequence is the ratio column: before
alignment the conditional term is a seventh of the marginal one ($0.14$); after
the strongest rung the two are nearly equal ($0.92$). The reverse direction
shows the same pattern more weakly, $0.29$ rising to $0.53$.

\textbf{The residual after alignment is therefore almost entirely conditional},
and no marginal alignment can act on it. This is the explanation the ladder
result requires: discrepancy does keep falling, by two orders of magnitude, but
the part that keeps falling was not the part limiting performance.

\paragraph{A falsifiable test of the decomposition.} A decomposition that only
describes is weak. Near-zero prior KL makes a prediction: a label-shift
correction should not help. We applied two --- black-box shift estimation and
the Saerens--Latinne--Decaestecker EM procedure [CITE: black-box shift
estimation / label shift correction, and the EM procedure for adjusting
classifier outputs to new priors --- two separate sources] --- and they do not
merely fail to help. They \textbf{hurt in 238 of 240} (record, estimator)
pairs, by $0.013$ to $0.24$ macro-F1. Both estimators recover a \emph{planted}
prior shift to within $0.05$ under test, so this is a property of the corpora
and not a broken implementation. Had either helped at a KL of $0.02$ nats, the
decomposition would have been wrong.

\subsection{CORAL degenerates to a mean shift at its selected regularisation}
\label{sec:results-eps}

CORAL's map is $M = C_s^{-1/2} C_t^{1/2}$ with each covariance regularised as
$C + \varepsilon\,\mathrm{tr}(C)/d \cdot I$. As $\varepsilon$ grows, both
regularised covariances approach a scaled identity and
\begin{equation}
  M \;\longrightarrow\; \sqrt{\mathrm{tr}(C_t)/\mathrm{tr}(C_s)} \; I ,
  \label{eq:coral-limit}
\end{equation}
so the transform $x \mapsto (x - \mu_s)M + \mu_t$ collapses to a global scalar
rescale plus a mean shift --- \texttt{mean\_shift} with one extra degree of
freedom. Table~\ref{tab:eps} and Figure~\ref{fig:eps} measure the approach.

\input{../tables/eps_asymptote}

\begin{figure}[tb]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{eps_asymptote.pdf}
  \caption{\texttt{source\_val} and target macro-F1 against CORAL's shrinkage
    $\varepsilon$. Dotted lines mark \texttt{mean\_shift}, the analytic limit of
    Equation~\eqref{eq:coral-limit}. Both curves saturate onto it.}
  \label{fig:eps}
\end{figure}

The relative distance from the fitted map to the nearest scaled identity,
$\lVert M - cI \rVert / \lVert M \rVert$, falls monotonically from $0.9254$ at
$\varepsilon = 10^{-4}$ to $0.0049$ at $\varepsilon = 1000$, and the fitted
scalar converges on the limit predicted by
Equation~\eqref{eq:coral-limit} (ratio $2.195 \rightarrow 1.000$).
\textbf{At $\varepsilon = 10$, the value selected on \texttt{source\_val}, the
map is already 86\% of the way to a pure scalar.} Empirically,
\texttt{source\_val} and target macro-F1 at $\varepsilon = 1000$ land within
$0.0061$ of \texttt{mean\_shift} in both directions.

Selecting CORAL's regularisation on source-side validation therefore drives it
continuously toward the simplest rung on the ladder. The elaborate method is
selected into being the simple one, which is a second, independent route to the
same conclusion as Section~\ref{sec:results-ladder} --- and one that involves no
classifier at all.

\subsection{Source-side validation cannot detect whether alignment is needed}
\label{sec:results-selection}

The gap in Table~\ref{tab:headline} is not a small constant overhead.
Figure~\ref{fig:selection} shows it per seed.

\begin{figure}[tb]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{validated_vs_oracle.pdf}
  \caption{Validated against oracle target macro-F1 for each seed, with the
    rung the protocol selected annotated on each validated bar. In the forward
    direction the protocol selects the unaligned condition on two of five
    seeds; on one of them the result falls below the chance floor.}
  \label{fig:selection}
\end{figure}

The mechanism is visible in the selection surface itself. Forward,
\texttt{source\_val} separates \texttt{none} ($0.7304$) from \texttt{zscore}
($0.7322$) by $0.0018$, while the same two rungs differ on target by $0.13$.
The criterion is effectively blind to the distinction that matters most.

The consequence is that selection falls back to \texttt{none} on two of five
forward seeds, returning target macro-F1 of $0.2803$ and $0.1508$. \textbf{The
second is below that pair's chance floor of $0.1665$.} A protocol that uses no
target labels --- the only protocol available to a practitioner --- therefore
produces, on one seed in five, a system worse than random guessing, while an
oracle over the same grid reaches $0.4555$.

The reverse direction selects an aligned rung on all five seeds
(\texttt{mean\_shift} four times, \texttt{zscore} once) and its gap is
correspondingly smaller, $0.0687$ against $0.1399$.

\subsection{Layer aggregation}
\label{sec:results-aggregation}

Comparisons A1 and A2 in Table~\ref{tab:primary} both survive correction.
Replacing final-layer pooling with a fixed mid-stack layer is worth $+0.0640$
$[+0.0541, +0.0748]$ forward and $+0.1439$ $[+0.1140, +0.1746]$ reverse;
replacing it with a learned softmax over all 13 states is worth $+0.0597$
$[+0.0504, +0.0702]$ and $+0.1096$ $[+0.0888, +0.1326]$.

These effects are larger than any difference \emph{among} the aligned rungs
($\le 0.0437$) and smaller than the decision to align at all ($+0.12$ to
$+0.20$). Final-layer pooling is both the common default and what the earlier
version of this work used.

\subsection{Frame dependence of the discrepancy--transfer relationship}
\label{sec:results-frames}

Alignment methods are commonly justified by the discrepancy they remove, which
presumes that the relationship between discrepancy and transfer is well defined.
Table~\ref{tab:frames} and Figure~\ref{fig:frames} test that presumption on a
13-layer sweep of 2340 runs.

\input{../tables/frames}

\begin{figure}[tb]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{frame_dependence.pdf}
  \caption{Left: Spearman $\rho$ between a layer's marginal discrepancy and its
    target macro-F1, in each of the two measurement geometries, for all 36
    (direction $\times$ backbone $\times$ rung) cells. Right: the case where the
    correct answer is known analytically --- as $\varepsilon$ grows the CORAL map
    provably tends to \texttt{mean\_shift}, and only the reference frame tracks
    it.}
  \label{fig:frames}
\end{figure}

Pooled over all cells and seeds, $\rho = -0.444$ $[-0.505, -0.383]$ in each
rung's own geometry and $\rho = +0.142$ $[+0.062, +0.221]$ in the fixed
reference frame. \textbf{The signs are opposite and neither interval covers
zero.} Eleven of the 36 individual cells disagree by the stricter per-cell test,
which requires both intervals to exclude zero and fall on opposite sides.

The two statistics are computed from the same features, the same layers and the
same target scores. Only the geometry in which the discrepancy is measured
differs.

\paragraph{Which frame is correct, in the one case where that is knowable.}
Sign disagreement alone does not tell us which frame to trust. The
$\varepsilon$ sweep supplies a case where the answer is known independently of
any measurement: Equation~\eqref{eq:coral-limit} says that as $\varepsilon$
grows, CORAL becomes a scalar rescale plus a mean shift. A discrepancy statistic
that is behaving correctly should therefore report large-$\varepsilon$ CORAL as
resembling \texttt{mean\_shift}. The reference-frame statistic does; the
own-geometry statistic does not, because its median-heuristic bandwidth adapts
to the very rescaling that distinguishes the two.

This does not make the reference frame universally right --- it is one arbitrary
frame among many, and it is more sensitive to uncorrected mean offsets. It does
establish that the choice is consequential and that at least one common way of
measuring discrepancy gives the wrong answer in a case with a known ground
truth.

\subsection{Direction, at matched training-set size}
\label{sec:results-direction}

Both directions are run at matched training-set size: CREMA-D's
\texttt{source\_train} is capped from 5972 to 988 so that any asymmetry cannot
be a data-quantity effect. Excluding the reduced-seed transformer arm, which
ran the primary direction only, target macro-F1 is $0.3429$
$[0.3265, 0.3627]$ for RAVDESS $\rightarrow$ CREMA-D against a chance floor of
$0.1665$, and $0.3987$ $[0.3793, 0.4155]$ for the reverse against $0.1656$.

Transfer into RAVDESS is therefore easier than transfer into CREMA-D even at
equal training-set size. RAVDESS's target test set is also far smaller (624
utterances against roughly 3680), which is why its intervals are wider
throughout this section.

\subsection{Blending toward the unaligned features}
\label{sec:results-blending}

Interpolating between aligned and original features,
$\alpha \cdot x_{\text{aligned}} + (1 - \alpha) \cdot x_{\text{original}}$, was
proposed in the earlier version of this work with $\alpha$ never searched. We
search it on the two rungs that move the features most.

Performance increases monotonically in $\alpha$ in the forward direction, from
$0.2987$ $[0.2746, 0.3188]$ at $\alpha = 0$ to $0.4012$ $[0.3802, 0.4186]$ at
$\alpha = 1$ for CORAL, and the reverse direction shows the same endpoints
($0.3154$ to $0.3833$) with a non-monotone interior. \textbf{The best setting of
the blending parameter is the one that turns blending off.} We therefore report
blending as a negative result rather than as a method.

This arm is smaller than the main grid --- 288 runs, one backbone, two seeds ---
and its intervals are correspondingly wider.

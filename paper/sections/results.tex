\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}

All target scores use macro-F1. Each score is compared with its pair-specific
chance floor. Intervals are 95\% paired cluster bootstrap intervals unless
stated otherwise.

\subsection{Alignment ladder}
\label{sec:results-ladder}

Table~\ref{tab:ladder} gives target macro-F1 and both marginal-discrepancy
measurements. Figure~\ref{fig:ladder} plots the target scores against the
own-geometry measurement.

\input{../tables/ladder}

\begin{figure}[tb]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{ladder.pdf}
  \caption{Target macro-F1 by alignment rung. Intervals are 95\% paired
    cluster bootstrap intervals. The chance floor is shown on the target-score
    axis. Marginal discrepancy uses each rung's own geometry and a log scale.
    Moving from \texttt{none} improves target macro-F1, but lower discrepancy
    does not produce a consistent target-score increase.}
  \label{fig:ladder}
\end{figure}

Moving from \texttt{none} to an aligned rung changes macro-F1 by $+0.1208$ to
$+0.1359$ in the forward direction and by $+0.1524$ to $+0.1961$ in the reverse
direction. All ten ladder-versus-\texttt{none} contrasts and both layer
contrasts survive Holm correction.

No aligned rung is shown to exceed \texttt{zscore}. The simultaneous upper
bound on an advantage over \texttt{zscore} is $+0.0148$ forward and $0.0000$
reverse. \texttt{mkmmd\_full} has the lowest marginal discrepancy in both
frames. In the reverse direction it has macro-F1 $0.3885$
$[0.3653, 0.4099]$, compared with $0.4321$ $[0.4070, 0.4547]$ for
\texttt{zscore}. The ordering of marginal discrepancy therefore does not match
the ordering of target macro-F1.

The MK-MMD optimiser reverts to its warm start in $64.9\%$ of
\texttt{mkmmd\_diag} runs and $55.2\%$ of \texttt{mkmmd\_full} runs. These
rows remain in the analysis and are labelled as warm-start fallbacks.

\subsection{Marginal and class-conditional diagnostics}
\label{sec:decomposition}

Table~\ref{tab:decomposition} and Figure~\ref{fig:decomposition} compare
label-prior KL, marginal discrepancy, and the class-conditional diagnostic.

\input{../tables/decomposition}

\begin{figure}[tb]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{decomposition.pdf}
  \caption{Marginal and class-conditional discrepancy by alignment rung.
    Both quantities are normalised by source half-split null values. Alignment
    reduces the marginal diagnostic at a higher rate than the
    class-conditional diagnostic in the forward direction. The ratio is
    class-conditional divided by marginal discrepancy.}
  \label{fig:decomposition}
\end{figure}

Prior KL is $0.02235$ nats forward and $0.02541$ nats reverse. From
\texttt{none} to \texttt{mkmmd\_full}, forward marginal discrepancy decreases
from $1496.1$ to $15.3$ and the class-conditional diagnostic decreases from
$215.1$ to $14.0$. The ratio changes from $0.14$ to $0.92$. Reverse, the ratio
changes from $0.29$ to $0.53$.

We also evaluated black-box shift estimation and the
Saerens--Latinne--Decaestecker EM correction
\cite{lipton2018bbse,saerens2002adjusting}. They reduce macro-F1 in 238 of 240
(record, estimator) cases by $0.013$ to $0.24$. Both recover an implanted
prior shift within $0.05$. The result is limited to these estimators and these
two corpora.

\subsection{CORAL shrinkage limit}
\label{sec:results-eps}

For $M=C_s^{-1/2}C_t^{1/2}$ with
$\widetilde C=C+\varepsilon\operatorname{tr}(C)I/d$,
\begin{equation}
 M\longrightarrow
 \sqrt{\operatorname{tr}(C_t)/\operatorname{tr}(C_s)}\,I
 \quad\text{as}\quad\varepsilon\longrightarrow\infty .
 \label{eq:coral-limit}
\end{equation}
CORAL therefore converges to a scalar-rescaled mean shift. Table~\ref{tab:eps}
and Figure~\ref{fig:eps} report the numerical probe.

\input{../tables/eps_asymptote}

\begin{figure}[tb]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{eps_asymptote.pdf}
  \caption{Source-validation and target macro-F1 by CORAL shrinkage
    $\varepsilon$. Dotted lines give \texttt{mean\_shift}. Equation
    \eqref{eq:coral-limit} predicts a scalar-rescaled mean shift as
    $\varepsilon$ increases.}
  \label{fig:eps}
\end{figure}

The relative distance from $M$ to its nearest scaled identity decreases from
$0.9254$ at $\varepsilon=10^{-4}$ to $0.0049$ at $\varepsilon=1000$. At the
source-selected value $\varepsilon=10$, the map is 86\% of the distance from
the low-shrinkage map to a scaled identity. Target macro-F1 at
$\varepsilon=1000$ is within $0.0061$ of \texttt{mean\_shift} in both
directions.

\subsection{Measurement geometry}
\label{sec:results-frames}

Figure~\ref{fig:frames} reports the correlation between layer-wise marginal
discrepancy and target macro-F1 in two frames.

\begin{figure}[tb]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{frame_dependence.pdf}
  \caption{Spearman correlation between layer-wise marginal discrepancy and
    target macro-F1. The left panel gives correlations in the own-geometry and
    reference-frame measurements. The right panel uses the CORAL shrinkage
    limit, for which Equation~\eqref{eq:coral-limit} gives the limiting map.}
  \label{fig:frames}
\end{figure}

The pooled correlation is $\rho=-0.444$ $[-0.505,-0.383]$ in own geometry and
$\rho=+0.142$ $[+0.062,+0.221]$ in the reference frame. Eleven of 36
(direction, backbone, rung) cells have intervals on opposite sides of zero.
The features, layers, and target scores are identical between the two
measurements. The frame is the only changed quantity. A discrepancy-transfer
claim must state its bandwidth rule and measurement basis.

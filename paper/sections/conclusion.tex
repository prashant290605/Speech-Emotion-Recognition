\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

A selection criterion can be leakage-free and still be uninformative. Avoiding
target labels makes a protocol procedurally valid; it does not make its score
sensitive to the decision being made.

For stationary-kernel classifiers we give a case where that gap is exact.
Translating source training and validation together preserves every
source-validation prediction while shifting the target decision function, so
the criterion is constant along a family of different target behaviours and no
amount of additional source-validation data resolves it. The frozen RBF-SVM
runs show the predicted equality in every matched cell, in both directions.
Logistic regression, a linear SVM and an MLP fall outside the fitting
assumptions and do not retain it, which is where the statement stops.

The same coordinate reasoning bounds what the surrounding comparison can show.
Relative transport, the classifier's coordinates and the kernel a discrepancy
is measured in are three separate objects, and an affine adaptation generally
changes all of them at once. A ladder ordered by moments matched is therefore a
comparison of pipelines, and a reported discrepancy reduction is defined only
relative to a stated basis and bandwidth. What this paper contributes is an
audit of what a criterion and a comparison can identify, with its assumptions
written down and its checks executable against recorded runs. For a
practitioner choosing a cross-corpus speech emotion recognition pipeline
without target labels, the consequence is concrete: an identical
source-validation score can conceal a different target decision function, so
that score is not by itself evidence about the adaptation choice. It is not a
recogniser, and it is not an unsupervised estimator of target risk.

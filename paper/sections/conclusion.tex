\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

Source-only validation can be leakage-free yet uninformative about a specific
adaptation decision. For stationary-kernel classifiers, translating source
training and validation preserves the validation problem while shifting the
target decision function. The frozen RBF-SVM runs exhibit the predicted
stored-score equality in every matched cell, with target macro-F1 gains of
$+0.1342$ forward and $+0.1969$ reverse on average. These are descriptive
results for RAVDESS and CREMA-D, not a universal benefit of mean matching.

The affine factorisation shows why this exact statement must not be extended
to the full alignment comparison. Relative transport, common classifier
coordinates and the discrepancy kernel are separate objects. Changing them
together yields a pipeline comparison, not an isolated test of how many
distribution moments need to match.

The contribution is a criterion audit with explicit mathematical assumptions,
an executable check against existing runs, and a framework for limiting the
claims drawn from affine SER comparisons. It is not a new hybrid recogniser
or an unsupervised solution to target-risk estimation. Establishing such a
solution would require evidence beyond the present study.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

On RAVDESS and CREMA-D, feature alignment increases cross-corpus macro-F1 by
$+0.12$ to $+0.20$ relative to unaligned features. Per-dimension
standardisation obtains this increase. CORAL and MK-MMD lower the reported
marginal discrepancy but do not show a further target-score increase.

The forward diagnostic changes from marginal and class-conditional values of
$1496.1$ and $215.1$ without alignment to $15.3$ and $14.0$ at the
\texttt{mkmmd\_full} endpoint. This pattern does not estimate $P(y\mid x)$,
but it shows that the class-conditional diagnostic does not decrease at the
same rate as the marginal objective. Source validation can also miss the
alignment decision, and the discrepancy-transfer correlation changes sign with
the measurement frame.

The practical reporting requirements are direct: give each target score with
its chance floor, distinguish source-selected scores from target-score oracles,
and state the geometry of each discrepancy measurement. The current results do
not provide an unsupervised target-side selection criterion for the remaining
cross-corpus variation.

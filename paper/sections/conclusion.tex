\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

Feature-space alignment improves cross-corpus speech emotion recognition by a
substantial margin --- $+0.12$ to $+0.20$ macro-F1 across the two directions
studied, against chance floors near $0.166$ --- and essentially all of that
margin is delivered by per-dimension standardisation. Matching covariance, or
matching higher-order structure through a learned map, buys nothing we can
measure: no aligned rung is shown to beat standardisation in either direction.

The reason is that these methods remove the wrong discrepancy. After alignment
the marginal term is at $0.010\times$ its unaligned value while the conditional
term is still at $0.07\times$, and the residual separating the two corpora is
$92\%$ conditional. Label shift, the other standard candidate, is excluded by
measurement rather than by assumption. A covariate-shift correction cannot
repair a conditional difference, and the elaborate rungs are not failing at
their objective --- they are succeeding at one that stopped binding after the
first step.

Three consequences bear on how the alignment literature's evidence should be
read. CORAL at its own selected regularisation has degenerated to a mean shift,
so a method may be reported as covariance matching while doing something
simpler. Source-side validation, the only selection protocol available without
target labels, cannot tell whether alignment is needed at all, and on one seed
returns a score below the chance floor; the cost of selection exceeds the
benefit of alignment. And the discrepancy--transfer correlation that motivates
the whole family reverses sign with the geometry it is measured in, so that
relationship is not identified until the measurement frame is stated --- which
is also our concrete recommendation to the field, since stating it costs
nothing.

The practical advice is short. Standardise. Spend the effort saved on the layer
the representation is taken from, which is worth more than any choice among
alignment methods, and on the selection protocol, which is worth more than
either. And report the floor beside every score, because the failure mode this
paper documents is invisible without it.

What we cannot offer is the thing the mechanism implies is needed: a target-side
signal sensitive to $P(y \mid x)$ that requires no target labels. Until
something plays that role, methods in this family will keep being selected by
criteria that cannot see the quantity that determines whether they work.

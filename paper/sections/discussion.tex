\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\subsection{A limitation of the criterion, not of its sample size}
\label{sec:discussion-selection}

The translation result explains a specific failure mode. A source-validation
criterion can assign identical evidence to different target decision functions.
In the matched RBF-SVM runs, both the selected validation score and the selected
hyperparameters are equal across the two arms, yet all target differences favour
mean shift. No amount of additional labelled source-validation data breaks the
symmetry in Proposition 1, because the symmetry holds for every sample. A
tie-break that always preferred mean shift would have succeeded on these
recorded pairs, but that preference is not learned from the source-validation
scores and is not justified for a new corpus pair by this study.

The broader selection failure in Section~\ref{sec:results-selection} is a
different and weaker phenomenon. Z-score changes anisotropic geometry and need
not preserve validation predictions; the small forward validation difference
there is empirical, not a consequence of Proposition 1. The two together give a
graded picture: the criterion is exactly blind on one axis, nearly blind on
another, and informative on a third (Section~\ref{sec:results-aggregation}).
Usefulness on one search axis does not imply usefulness on another.

\subsection{Why the result does not extend to all affine adaptation}
\label{sec:discussion-practice}

The factorisation prevents a tempting generalisation. Standardisation changes
relative moments \emph{and} the common feature coordinates the head works in.
The latter turns an isotropic coefficient penalty into an anisotropic one when
expressed in original target coordinates, and a finite scalar search does not
generally compensate for it. The existing grid therefore cannot decide how much
of standardisation's gain is due to moment transport, to conditioning, or to
their interaction with optimisation. Its scores remain valid measurements of
the pipelines that were evaluated.

The same argument applies to the measurement side. A discrepancy is defined
relative to a basis and a bandwidth, and Section~\ref{sec:results-frames} shows
two defensible protocols giving opposite pooled signs on the same runs and the
same target scores. The CORAL limit is informative for a third reason: it
specifies the map as shrinkage increases, independently of any measured MMD.
That limit retains a source-only scalar factor, so it is not exactly the
mean-shift map unless that factor is unity, and the observed score proximity is
empirical rather than implied.

\subsection{What the executable audit gives a researcher}
\label{sec:discussion-testing}

Before interpreting a feature-alignment comparison, record both domain maps,
the head's penalty and intercept convention, and the diagnostic kernel. Check
whether a candidate adaptation leaves the validation predictions invariant; for
a stationary kernel and a source-only translation, the check is arithmetic and
needs no training run. For coordinate-equivalent pipelines, either transform the
objective and the kernel consistently or state that the comparison includes
their change. When reporting a discrepancy reduction, state the basis and the
bandwidth rule. The affine-identity tests and the ledger matcher released with
this paper make each of these checks executable against recorded runs.

\subsection{What it does not give them}
\label{sec:discussion-limits}

This audit detects an uninformative criterion; it does not supply the missing
target-risk information. Existing unsupervised selectors use additional signals
or assumptions \cite{you2019selection,hu2024selection}; they were not evaluated
here, so we cannot rank them, and the translation result does not invalidate
them. Nothing here recommends mean shift as an adaptation method: its advantage
in these cells is descriptive, measured against an unaligned baseline in one
corpus pair, and the criterion under study cannot detect it. Likewise, neither
class-conditional MMD nor a small empirical prior KL identifies the cause of
transfer error. A direct conditional-shift study would need adequate labelled
support and control of corpus, speaker and elicitation differences; paired
recordings are one possible design, not a mathematical requirement.

\subsection{Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}

\paragraph{Empirical scope.} The evidence comes from RAVDESS and CREMA-D,
both acted English speech. It does not establish the frequency or magnitude
of validation invariance in spontaneous, multilingual or additional-corpus
SER. Source-training size is matched, but validation, adaptation and test
sizes differ by direction, so the directional asymmetry cannot be assigned to
one corpus property. The target scores are not state-of-the-art claims.

\paragraph{Analytic scope.} The kernel proposition assumes matching candidate
sets and fitting rules determined by invariant matrices. The linear-objective
argument assumes an unpenalised intercept and corresponding optima. Finite
solvers can violate numerical equality, as the logistic-regression, linear-SVM
and MLP rows of the audit show; those rows delimit the proposition rather than
testing it. These conditions exclude a blanket claim for neural training or for
domain-wise z-score. The affine lemma requires an invertible target map.

\paragraph{Retrospective evidence.} The translation audit was designed after
the runs existed. It reports all eligible matches and does not tune on target
scores, but it is not a prospectively registered experiment. The ledger stores
winning validation scores, not the predictions for every search candidate, so
it verifies the recorded equalities rather than every numerical equality
predicted for an ideal full search surface. Cells share seeds and speakers;
their count is not a count of independent replications.

\paragraph{Other experimental arms.} The transformer has two seeds in the
forward direction only. It is included where available in the full-grid
headline and ladder summaries, which therefore have unequal forward candidate
sets across seeds, and it is excluded from the translation audit. MK-MMD falls
back to its warm start in $64.9\%$ of diagonal-map runs and $55.2\%$ of
full-map runs, so those results evaluate a complete finite procedure rather
than every possible optimiser or affine MK-MMD solution. The
measurement-protocol correlations are descriptive sensitivity analyses, not
independent evidence for a causal mechanism.

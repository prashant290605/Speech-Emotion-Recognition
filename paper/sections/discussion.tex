\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\subsection{A limitation of the criterion, not of its sample size}
\label{sec:discussion-selection}

The translation result identifies a specific failure mode: a source-validation
criterion can assign identical evidence to different target decision
functions. No amount of additional labelled source-validation data breaks the
symmetry in Proposition 1, because the symmetry holds for every sample. A
tie-break that always preferred mean shift would have succeeded on the recorded
pairs, but such a preference is not learned from the source-validation scores
and is not justified for a new corpus pair by this study. The broader selection
failure in Section~\ref{sec:results-selection} is a different and weaker
phenomenon. Z-score standardisation changes the anisotropic geometry and does
not necessarily preserve validation predictions, so the small forward
validation difference is empirical rather than a consequence of
Proposition 1. Together, the results give a graded picture. The criterion is
exactly blind on one axis, nearly blind on another, and informative on a third
(Section~\ref{sec:results-aggregation}).

\subsection{Scope of the affine factorisation}
\label{sec:discussion-practice}

The factorisation prevents a tempting generalisation. Standardisation changes
both the relative moments and the common coordinates in which the head is
fitted. Expressed in the original target coordinates, the change of
coordinates turns an isotropic coefficient penalty into an anisotropic one,
and a finite scalar search does not generally compensate for it. The existing
grid therefore cannot determine how much of the gain from standardisation is
due to moment transport, to conditioning, or to their interaction with
optimisation, although its scores remain valid measurements of the evaluated
pipelines. The same argument applies to the measurement side, where two
defensible protocols give opposite pooled signs on the same runs and target
scores (Section~\ref{sec:results-frames}).

\subsection{Practical implications of the executable audit}
\label{sec:discussion-testing}

Before interpreting a feature-alignment comparison, researchers should record
both domain maps, the penalty and intercept convention of the classifier head,
and the diagnostic kernel. They should then check whether a candidate
adaptation leaves the validation predictions invariant. For a stationary kernel
and a source-only translation, this check is arithmetic and requires no
training run. For coordinate-equivalent pipelines, either the objective and the
kernel should be transformed consistently, or the report should state that the
comparison includes their change. A reported discrepancy reduction should state
its basis and bandwidth rule. The affine-identity tests and the ledger matcher
released with this paper make each of these checks executable against recorded
runs.

\subsection{Limitations of the audit}
\label{sec:limitations}

The audit identifies an uninformative selection criterion, but it does not
provide the missing information about target risk. Unsupervised selectors that
use additional signals or assumptions \cite{you2019selection,hu2024selection}
were not evaluated, and the translation result does not invalidate them. The
analytical results also depend on explicit assumptions. The kernel proposition
requires matched candidate sets and fitting rules determined by invariant
kernel matrices, the linear-objective argument requires an unpenalised
intercept, and the affine lemma requires an invertible target map. Neural
training and domain-wise z-score standardisation therefore fall outside the
scope of the exact result.

The empirical evidence comes from two acted English corpora, so the frequency
and magnitude of validation invariance in spontaneous, multilingual or other
corpora remain to be established. The audit is also retrospective. Although it
reports all eligible matches without using target scores, it was designed after
the runs were completed, and no prospective replication on a held-out corpus
pair is reported. The matched cells share seeds and speakers, so they are not
independent replications. Finally, the Transformer arm was run with two seeds
in one direction only, the MK-MMD results include the fallback to the warm
start, and the measurement-protocol correlations are descriptive.

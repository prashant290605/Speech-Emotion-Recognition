\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\subsection{A limitation of the criterion, not only its sample size}
\label{sec:discussion-selection}

The translation result explains a specific failure mode. A source-validation
criterion can assign identical evidence to different target decision functions.
In the matched RBF-SVM runs, both the selected validation score and the selected
hyperparameters are equal across the two arms, yet all target differences
favour mean shift. A larger validation set cannot break the exact symmetry in
Proposition 1. A tie-break that always prefers mean shift would succeed on
these recorded pairs, but that preference is not learned from the source
validation scores and is not justified for a new corpus pair by this study.

This differs from the broader \texttt{none}--\texttt{zscore} result. Z-score
changes anisotropic geometry and need not preserve validation predictions.
The small forward validation difference in Section~\ref{sec:results-selection}
is empirical, not a consequence of Proposition 1. Source-side validation can
also be informative about layer depth. There is no contradiction: usefulness
on one search axis does not imply usefulness on another.

\subsection{What the alignment comparison does not identify}
\label{sec:discussion-practice}

The factorisation prevents a tempting causal interpretation. Standardisation
changes relative moments and the common feature coordinates used by the head.
The latter changes an isotropic coefficient penalty into an anisotropic one
when expressed in original target coordinates. A finite scalar search does
not generally compensate for this. The existing grid therefore cannot decide
how much of standardisation's gain is due to moment transport, conditioning,
or their interaction with optimisation. Its scores remain valid measurements
of the evaluated pipelines.

The CORAL limit is informative for a different reason. It specifies the map
as shrinkage increases, independently of its measured MMD. The limit retains
a source-only scalar factor; it is not exactly the mean-shift map unless that
factor is unity. The observed score proximity is empirical. It does not prove
that classifier geometry is irrelevant or that source validation must select
the same shrinkage on another dataset.

\subsection{A usable audit rather than an untested remedy}
\label{sec:discussion-testing}

Before interpreting a feature-alignment comparison, record both domain maps,
the head's penalty and intercept convention, and the diagnostic kernel. Check
whether a candidate adaptation leaves the validation predictions invariant.
For coordinate-equivalent pipelines, either transform the objective and
kernel consistently or state that the comparison includes their change.
The accompanying affine-identity tests and ledger matcher make these checks
executable without training an additional model.

This audit detects an uninformative criterion; it does not supply the missing
target-risk information. Existing unsupervised selectors use additional
signals or assumptions \cite{you2019selection,hu2024selection}. They were not
evaluated here, so we cannot rank them or claim that the translation result
invalidates them. Likewise, neither class-conditional MMD nor a small empirical
prior KL identifies the cause of transfer error. A direct conditional-shift
study would need adequate labelled support and control of corpus, speaker and
elicitation differences; paired recordings are one possible design, not a
mathematical requirement.

\subsection{Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}

\paragraph{Empirical scope.} The evidence comes from RAVDESS and CREMA-D,
both acted English speech. It does not establish the frequency or magnitude
of validation invariance in spontaneous, multilingual or additional-corpus
SER. Source-training size is matched, but validation, adaptation and test
sizes differ by direction. The directional asymmetry cannot be assigned to
one corpus property. The target scores are not state-of-the-art claims.

\paragraph{Analytic scope.} The kernel proposition assumes matching candidate
sets and fitting rules determined by invariant matrices. The linear-objective
argument assumes an unpenalised intercept and corresponding optima. Finite
solvers can violate numerical equality, as the logistic-regression audit
shows. These conditions exclude a blanket claim for neural training or
domain-wise z-score. The affine lemma requires an invertible target map.

\paragraph{Retrospective evidence.} The translation audit was designed after
the runs existed. It reports all eligible matches and does not tune on target
scores, but it is not a prospectively registered experiment. The ledger stores
winning validation scores, not the predictions for every search candidate.
It therefore verifies the recorded equalities, not every numerical equality
predicted for an ideal full search surface. Cells share seeds and speakers;
their count is not a count of independent replications.

\paragraph{Other experimental arms.} The Transformer has two seeds in the
forward direction only and is not pooled with the five-seed families.
MK-MMD falls back to its warm start in $64.9\%$ of diagonal-map runs and
$55.2\%$ of full-map runs. Its finite-sample acceptance check uses unpenalised
MMD, not the penalised training objective. The results evaluate this complete
procedure, not every possible optimiser or affine MK-MMD solution. The
measurement-protocol correlations are descriptive sensitivity analyses, not
independent evidence for a causal mechanism.

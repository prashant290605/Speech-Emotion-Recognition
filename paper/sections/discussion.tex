\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

The ladder isolates the observed transfer gain. Per-dimension standardisation
adds $+0.12$ to $+0.20$ macro-F1 relative to \texttt{none}. The simultaneous
upper bound on any evaluated aligned rung's advantage over \texttt{zscore} is
$+0.0148$ forward and $0.0000$ reverse. CORAL and MK-MMD reduce marginal
discrepancy beyond \texttt{zscore}, but that reduction does not yield a
consistent macro-F1 increase.

The diagnostics describe one feature of this plateau. In the forward direction,
the marginal statistic decreases to $0.010$ of its unaligned value while the
class-conditional statistic decreases to $0.07$. The statistic compares
$P(x\mid y)$, not $P(y\mid x)$, so it does not identify a causal mechanism.
The prior KL values are $0.02235$ and $0.02541$ nats. BBSE and EM correction
reduce performance in 238 of 240 cases. These measurements rule out neither
conditional shift nor other sources of transfer error.

Source validation is useful for classifier tuning, but it provides no reliable
signal for the forward alignment decision in these runs. Its $0.0018$
difference between \texttt{none} and \texttt{zscore} contrasts with a $0.13$
target difference. The validated-oracle gap is $0.1399$ forward. Reporting the
oracle without its selection status would report an upper bound as an
achievable target score.

The geometry result concerns measurement, not an alignment preference. The
same layer-wise runs yield Spearman correlations of $-0.444$ and $+0.142$ under
two stated frames. Researchers should report the kernel bandwidth rule and the
basis used to measure discrepancy. A numerical discrepancy reduction without
those choices is not comparable across feature transformations.

\subsection{Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}

The study covers two English corpora and two transfer directions. Both corpora
contain acted speech. The findings do not establish the same pattern for
spontaneous speech, another language, or a pair with a larger label-prior
divergence. RAVDESS target tests contain 624 utterances; CREMA-D target tests
contain 3677 to 3690 utterances. Reverse-direction intervals are therefore
wider. Training-set size is matched, but adaptation and test-set sizes differ.
The MK-MMD rungs also fall back to their initial maps in $64.9\%$ and $55.2\%$
of diagonal and full runs. The evaluation therefore tests this optimisation
budget and these affine maps, not every possible MMD implementation.

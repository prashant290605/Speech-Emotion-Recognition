\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

Speech emotion recognisers trained on one corpus can lose accuracy on another
corpus. Feature-space alignment is a standard response. It transforms source
features to reduce a discrepancy with unlabelled target features before
classification. Standardisation, CORAL, and MMD-based methods are established
examples.

This study tests those methods under one speaker-disjoint protocol on RAVDESS
and CREMA-D. The alignment ladder improves target macro-F1 by $+0.1208$ to
$+0.1359$ for RAVDESS $\rightarrow$ CREMA-D and by $+0.1524$ to $+0.1961$ for
CREMA-D $\rightarrow$ RAVDESS. The chance floors are $0.1665$ and $0.1656$.
Per-dimension standardisation obtains the observed improvement. No subsequent
rung is shown to exceed it.

The study then asks why lower marginal discrepancy does not produce a higher
target score. In the forward direction, the \texttt{mkmmd\_full} endpoint
reduces the marginal diagnostic from $1496.1$ to $15.3$ and the
class-conditional diagnostic from $215.1$ to $14.0$. Their ratio changes from
$0.14$ to $0.92$. This diagnostic compares $P(x\mid y)$ across corpora. It is
not an additive decomposition and does not estimate $P(y\mid x)$. Label-prior
KL is $0.02235$ and $0.02541$ nats. Two label-shift corrections reduce macro-F1
in 238 of 240 evaluated cases, while recovering a planted prior shift.

The protocol also exposes two evaluation problems. Source validation separates
\texttt{none} and \texttt{zscore} by $0.0018$ in the forward direction, whereas
their target scores differ by $0.13$. It selects the unaligned condition in two
of five seeds, including one target score of $0.1508$, below the $0.1665$ chance
floor. Further, the correlation between discrepancy and transfer is $-0.444$
in each method's own geometry and $+0.142$ in a fixed reference geometry. The
measurement frame changes the sign of the result.

\paragraph{Contributions.}
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{An alignment ladder with controlled selection.} Alignment adds
    $+0.12$ to $+0.20$ macro-F1, and no rung is shown to improve on
    per-dimension standardisation (Section~\ref{sec:results-ladder}).
  \item \textbf{A shift diagnostic.} Marginal discrepancy falls to $0.010$
    times its unaligned value, while the class-conditional diagnostic falls to
    $0.07$ times its unaligned value (Section~\ref{sec:decomposition}).
  \item \textbf{A CORAL limit.} With increasing shrinkage, CORAL converges to
    a scalar-rescaled mean shift; the target scores at $\varepsilon=1000$ are
    within $0.0061$ of \texttt{mean\_shift} (Section~\ref{sec:results-eps}).
  \item \textbf{A selection result.} Source-side validation can fail to select
    alignment even when alignment changes target macro-F1 by $0.13$
    (Section~\ref{sec:results-selection}).
  \item \textbf{A geometry result.} The discrepancy-transfer correlation
    reverses sign between two stated measurement frames
    (Section~\ref{sec:results-frames}).
\end{enumerate}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

A speech emotion recogniser trained on one corpus can perform poorly on
another. A standard response is to align the two feature distributions before
training. Per-dimension standardisation, CORAL, MMD minimisation, and related
methods form a well-established family. Studies usually support a member of
this family by showing improved transfer and lower measured discrepancy between
the domains.

This controlled RAVDESS and CREMA-D case study runs that family end to end on
one fixed protocol. It asks what the observed improvement consists of. Alignment
adds between $+0.1208$ and $+0.1359$
macro-F1 for RAVDESS $\rightarrow$ CREMA-D and between $+0.1524$ and $+0.1961$
for CREMA-D $\rightarrow$ RAVDESS, against chance baselines of $0.1665$ and
$0.1656$. But essentially all of it is available from per-dimension
standardisation. The diagnostics below test one explanation for why further
marginal discrepancy reduction does not yield a further transfer gain.

\paragraph{The diagnostic.} Measuring label shift, marginal discrepancy and a
class-conditional discrepancy shows what the ladder is doing. Alignment drives
the marginal term down to $0.010\times$ its unaligned value while the
class-conditional term falls only to $0.07\times$. These diagnostics use
different null normalisers, so their numerical ratio is descriptive rather than
a decomposition. The observed pattern indicates that the class-conditional
diagnostic does not decline as quickly as the marginal quantity the ladder
optimises. The observed prior difference is small: the KL is
$0.02235$ and $0.02541$ nats, and the standard corrections examined here
\emph{hurt} in 238 of 240 cases while the same estimators recover a planted
shift. This does not establish that label shift is absent, because those
corrections assume stable class-conditionals.

The evaluated affine maps reduce their marginal objectives without a
corresponding target-macro-F1 gain. \texttt{mkmmd\_full} reaches the lowest
marginal discrepancy in both measurement geometries. Its documented optimiser
fallback rate means this is evidence about the evaluated optimisation budget,
not every possible MK-MMD map. The diagnostic does not establish why the
discrepancy reduction does not improve transfer.

\paragraph{What follows from it.} Three further results fall out, each of which
qualifies the interpretation of this controlled corpus-pair result.

CORAL, at the regularisation its own source-side validation selects, approaches
the simpler rung. We derive analytically that as the shrinkage $\varepsilon$
grows the CORAL map tends to a scalar rescale plus a mean shift, and confirm it:
at the selected $\varepsilon = 10$ the map is already $86\%$ of the way to a
pure scalar, and at $\varepsilon = 1000$ its scores land within $0.0061$ of
\texttt{mean\_shift} in both directions.

In the forward direction, source-side validation can miss whether alignment is
needed. It separates \texttt{none} from \texttt{zscore} by $0.0018$ where the
target separates them by $0.13$. On this decision it is nearly blind to the
target ordering, selecting the unaligned configuration on 2 of 5 seeds. One of
those seeds returns $0.1508$, \textbf{below the $0.1665$ chance baseline}. The
protocol touched no target labels at any point. The gap between
validated and oracle selection, $0.1399$ $[0.0231, 0.2568]$ forward, is the
oracle gap. It is larger than the observed alignment gain, but it also includes
target-set maximisation and search-space effects.

Finally, the relationship between discrepancy and transfer is not invariant to
measurement geometry. Measured in each method's own geometry the
correlation is $\rho = -0.444$ $[-0.505, -0.383]$; measured in a single fixed
reference frame, on the same features, layers and scores, it is $+0.142$
$[+0.062, +0.221]$. The signs are opposite and neither interval covers zero.
Which one a study obtains is fixed by a measurement choice made before any
transfer result is seen, and often left implicit. The $\varepsilon$ sweep
supplies a case where the limiting map is known independently, and the two
frames disagree about its proximity to that limit.

\paragraph{What this paper is and is not.} It is not a new alignment method and
does not report a new best score; the absolute numbers here are modest. It is a
controlled analysis of one English acted-speech corpus pair. The protocol makes
the analysis checkable: comparisons were pre-specified before the frozen
confirmatory grid, splits are speaker-disjoint, each score carries a chance
baseline, classifier families have equal search budgets, and validated and
oracle scores are distinguished explicitly. The accompanying materials record
the frozen protocol, provenance, and withdrawn interim claims.

\paragraph{Contributions.}
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{A diagnostic for the alignment plateau.} Marginal discrepancy
    falls to $0.010\times$ its unaligned value while the class-conditional
    diagnostic falls to $0.07\times$. Observed prior differences are small, and
    the standard corrections examined here hurt in 238 of 240 cases while
    recovering a planted shift.
    (Section~\ref{sec:decomposition})
  \item \textbf{The gain is the first rung in this case study.} Alignment is
    worth $+0.12$ to $+0.20$, and no aligned rung is shown to beat
    per-dimension standardisation in either direction.
    (Section~\ref{sec:results-ladder})
  \item \textbf{CORAL approaches a scalar-rescaled mean shift as shrinkage
    grows.} Derived analytically and confirmed to within $0.0061$.
    (Section~\ref{sec:results-eps})
  \item \textbf{Forward source-side validation can miss whether alignment is
    needed}, and once returns a score below the chance baseline.
    (Section~\ref{sec:results-selection})
  \item \textbf{The relationship between discrepancy and transfer reverses
    sign with the measurement frame}, so it is not invariant until the frame is
    specified. (Section~\ref{sec:results-frames})
\end{enumerate}

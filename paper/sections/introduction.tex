\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

Cross-corpus speech emotion recognition requires choosing a model without
labelled examples from the deployment corpus. Source-side validation avoids
using target test labels, but avoiding leakage does not make the criterion
informative about every adaptation decision. This paper asks a narrower
question: can a valid source-validation protocol be unable to distinguish an
adaptation operation that changes target predictions?

For source translations and stationary-kernel classifiers, the answer is
exact. Translating source training and validation together preserves their
kernel matrices. Target vectors are not translated, so their predictions can
change. The validation criterion is constant along a family of different
target decision functions. This is not sampling noise that a larger source
validation set necessarily removes.

The existing RAVDESS and CREMA-D results contain a direct test. In each
direction, all 30 matched RBF-SVM cells have exactly equal stored validation
scores and selected hyperparameters for \texttt{none} and
\texttt{mean\_shift}. Mean shift improves target macro-F1 in every matched
cell. The mean differences are $+0.1342$ forward and $+0.1969$ reverse.
These are retrospective summaries of frozen runs, not new training experiments
or independent replications across cells sharing a seed.

The result must not be extended to every alignment operation. Domain-wise
standardisation changes both domains' coordinates. CORAL and the evaluated
affine MK-MMD maps transform only the source. We factor such maps into relative
domain transport and a common coordinate change, then express the classifier
penalty and discrepancy kernel in the same coordinates. This identifies a
second limitation: the comparison commonly described as a ladder of matched
moments also changes the effective learner. The recorded scores compare
pipelines, not isolated moment-matching mechanisms.

Neither the general failure of marginal alignment nor the difficulty of
unsupervised model selection is new
\cite{zhao2019invariant,you2019selection,ericsson2023practices,hu2024selection}.
Our contribution is an explicit invariance test for one selection criterion,
its manifestation in a controlled SER ledger, and the boundaries that prevent
that result from being misread as a universal claim about alignment.

\paragraph{Contributions.}
\begin{enumerate}
 \item We establish a source-translation invariance for stationary-kernel
   validation and state its implications and solver assumptions
   (Section~\ref{sec:translation-theory}).
 \item We provide an executable, read-only audit of matched frozen runs. It
   tests exact equality without rounding or target-based cell selection and
   includes logistic regression as a numerical contrast
   (Section~\ref{sec:results-translation}).
 \item We separate relative transport, classifier geometry and measurement
   geometry. This gives explicit limits on interpreting the broader affine
   comparison and its discrepancy diagnostics
   (Sections~\ref{sec:affine-factorisation} and~\ref{sec:results-frames}).
\end{enumerate}

The paper does not propose a recogniser or claim state-of-the-art performance.
The analytic statements have explicit assumptions; the empirical evidence is
limited to two English acted-speech corpora. The mathematical audit was
developed after the experimental ledger was frozen. The original comparisons
retain their recorded pre-specification status, but the present research
question is retrospective.

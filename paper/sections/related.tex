\section{Related work}
\label{sec:related}

\subsection{Cross-corpus speech emotion recognition}
\label{sec:related-crosscorpus}

That emotion recognition degrades when the test corpus is not the training
corpus is old enough to have been systematised: cross-corpus evaluation was
already being posed as a question about variances across corpora, and about
strategies for absorbing them, fifteen years ago \cite{schuller2010cross}. The
responses since have been broadly of three kinds. Transfer learning and
fine-tuning move a model trained on one corpus toward another
\cite{naderi2023cross}. Multi-task and auxiliary-objective formulations try to
learn a representation that is not corpus-specific in the first place
\cite{fu2023cross}. Training-strategy work changes what the model sees rather
than what it computes \cite{pastor2023cross}. Domain adaptation proper; the
family this paper is about; appears in both adversarial \cite{latif2023self}
and feature-alignment \cite{naeeni2025feature} forms, the latter on the same
class of self-supervised encoder used here.

Most of this work is evaluated on some subset of a small pool of corpora, of
which IEMOCAP \cite{busso2008iemocap}, RAVDESS \cite{livingstone2018ryerson}
and CREMA-D \cite{cao2014crema} are the most frequent. The reported quantity is
usually a gain: some method, on some corpus pair, over some baseline. In the
studies just cited, the baseline, corpus pair, label mapping and split policy
differ, so their gains are not numerically commensurable. This motivates a
controlled within-protocol comparison of how much each step of an alignment
ladder contributes.

\subsection{Feature-space alignment under covariate shift}
\label{sec:related-alignment}

Alignment methods rest on a covariate-shift assumption: the marginal feature
distribution differs between corpora while the conditional distribution of the
label given the features does not \cite{shimodaira2000covariate}. One commonly
invoked form of theoretical support is a bound in which target error is
controlled by source error plus a divergence between the domains; and, in
the standard result, that divergence is
\emph{classifier-induced}: it is defined over the symmetric difference of the
hypothesis class, and so depends on which hypotheses the learner can express
\cite{bendavid2010theory}. This distinction is easy to lose
and matters here: a kernel MMD at a fixed bandwidth is not a
hypothesis-dependent quantity, so reducing it alone does not establish a
reduction in that bound's divergence or in target error.

The methods form a natural ladder, ordered by how much structure they match.
Per-dimension standardisation matches first and second moments separately in
each dimension but not their cross-dimension terms. CORAL matches the full
second-order structure by whitening the source and recolouring it to the target
covariance in a closed form \cite{sun2016coral} and as a differentiable training
loss \cite{sun2016deep}. MMD-based methods minimise a kernel two-sample
statistic \cite{gretton2012kernel}; deep adaptation networks use multi-kernel
mean-embedding matching in hidden layers \cite{long2015dan}. Adversarial
approaches use a learned domain discriminator and gradient reversal rather than
a closed-form statistic \cite{ganin2016dann}.

This paper is deliberately narrower than a survey of those methods. It fixes
the candidate rungs, their tuning budgets, the selection rule and the geometry
in which their discrepancy is measured, then reports where the observed gain
appears. The conditional and label-shift diagnostics are reported as diagnostics
of this corpus pair, not as a test of every assumption needed for domain
adaptation.

\subsection{Self-supervised representations, and where emotion lives in them}
\label{sec:related-ssl}

Self-supervised encoders \cite{baevski2020wav2vec,hsu2021hubert,chen2022wavlm}
supply the features for most current SER systems, including the ones cited
above. How a stack of transformer layers is reduced to a single vector is a
choice, and the two common defaults are the final layer and a task-learned
weighted sum over layers \cite{yang2021superb}. Layer-wise probing has shown
that information in self-supervised speech representations changes across depth
\cite{pasad2021layer}; the present study tests the downstream consequence for
cross-corpus emotion recognition rather than assuming it from phonetic or
lexical probes.

Layer choice and alignment are, however, usually studied apart. That separation
is why a result like ours; that the layer decision is worth more than any
choice \emph{among} alignment methods; is available to be found rather than
already known.

\subsection{Protocol: selection, floors, and what a reported number means}
\label{sec:related-protocol}

Cross-corpus transfer has no labelled target data available for model selection,
which makes selection part of the evaluation protocol rather than a detail of
implementation. Optimising any finite-sample selection criterion can introduce
selection bias into performance evaluation \cite{cawley2010selection}. We
therefore distinguish a source-validation-selected score from the maximum target
score in the grid, which is labelled as an oracle rather than reported as an
achievable result.

Two further protocol choices bear on interpretation. We report each macro-F1
beside its analytic chance and majority-class floors, because the realised test
prior makes those floors pair-specific. We also use speaker-disjoint partitions
throughout, so a target speaker is absent from both model fitting and alignment.
Benchmark efforts have begun to standardise related evaluation choices
\cite{ma2024emobox}.

\subsection{Position of this work}
\label{sec:related-position}

This paper is not a new alignment method and does not report a new best score.
It takes an existing and widely used family; the ladder of
Section~\ref{sec:related-alignment}; runs all of it under one fixed protocol,
and asks three questions the work above does not answer within one controlled
protocol: where the gain actually comes from, whether a marginal diagnostic
tracks transfer, and whether the quantity the family optimises is stable under
its measurement frame. The answers
are, in order: the first rung; the measured class-conditional diagnostic does
not fall as quickly as the marginal statistic; and not until a measurement
frame is fixed. The nearest
precedent is the benchmarking strand represented by \cite{ma2024emobox}, which
shares the aim of making cross-corpus numbers comparable but pursues it by
standardising the evaluation rather than by decomposing the effect.

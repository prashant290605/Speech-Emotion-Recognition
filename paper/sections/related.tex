\section{Related work}
\label{sec:related}

\subsection{Cross-corpus speech emotion recognition}
\label{sec:related-crosscorpus}

Cross-corpus speech emotion recognition (SER) and strategies for handling
corpus variation are established research problems \cite{schuller2010cross}. Approaches include transfer
learning \cite{naderi2023cross}, multi-task learning \cite{fu2023cross},
training-strategy changes \cite{pastor2023cross}, adversarial adaptation
\cite{latif2023self}, and feature alignment \cite{naeeni2025feature}.
A recent \emph{Speech Communication} study combines class-sensitive feature
dictionaries with an adversarial discriminator in a semi-supervised network
\cite{zhang2025sdan}. It learns representations with additional target
supervision, unlike the source-only affine maps examined in this work.

Frequently used corpora include IEMOCAP \cite{busso2008iemocap}, RAVDESS
\cite{livingstone2018ryerson} and CREMA-D \cite{cao2014crema}. Their label
mappings, speakers, elicitation procedures and evaluation protocols differ, so
reported scores cannot be ranked without controlling those choices. EmoBox
\cite{ma2024emobox} addresses reproducible cross-corpus evaluation. Rather than
adding another benchmark, our work examines the information available to the
model-selection criterion.

\subsection{Unsupervised model selection under domain shift}
\label{sec:related-protocol}

Selecting hyperparameters on target test scores gives an optimistic,
non-deployable estimate, and selection bias is a general evaluation issue
\cite{cawley2010selection}. Deep Embedded Validation \cite{you2019selection},
the study of validation practice by \cite{ericsson2023practices} and the
reliability study of \cite{hu2024selection} already address model selection in
domain adaptation. Their criteria use or evaluate signals beyond the
source-validation score of a single candidate. The invariance reported in this
work does not invalidate those criteria, nor does it imply that no unsupervised
selector can work. It characterises what one widely used and
safe-by-construction criterion cannot detect.

\subsection{Alignment and the limits of marginal matching}
\label{sec:related-alignment}

Under covariate shift, the feature marginal changes while the conditional
label distribution is stable \cite{shimodaira2000covariate}. Domain adaptation
also studies settings outside that assumption. A standard target-error bound
contains source error, a domain divergence and the error of the best joint
hypothesis \cite{bendavid2010theory}. Its divergence is classifier-induced,
defined over the symmetric difference of the hypothesis class. Reducing a
kernel two-sample statistic alone therefore does not necessarily reduce that
divergence or the joint-error term.

CORAL matches covariance structure \cite{sun2016coral}, and Deep CORAL makes
this a representation-learning objective \cite{sun2016deep}. The maximum mean
discrepancy (MMD) compares kernel mean embeddings
\cite{gretton2012kernel}, and deep adaptation networks
apply multi-kernel matching to learned features \cite{long2015dan}.
Domain-adversarial networks instead use a domain classifier
\cite{ganin2016dann}. These are different adaptation objectives, not a nested
sequence of interventions on moments alone. Marginal invariance with low source
error is already known to be insufficient for successful adaptation
\cite{zhao2019invariant}, and we do not claim discovery of that failure. Our
exact result concerns a validation symmetry within a specified family of source
transformations, and the affine factorisation clarifies why it does not
automatically apply to the other conditions.

\subsection{Speech representations and layer choice}
\label{sec:related-ssl}

HuBERT, wav2vec 2.0 and WavLM provide the pretrained speech representations
used in this work \cite{hsu2021hubert,baevski2020wav2vec,chen2022wavlm}.
Final-layer pooling and learned layer aggregation are established choices
\cite{yang2021superb}, and layer-wise probing shows that the information a
speech encoder represents changes with depth \cite{pasad2021layer}. In this
work, layer comparisons provide experimental context for the criterion audit.
We do not claim as new that intermediate layers can be useful. The nearest
methodological precedents therefore concern model selection for domain
adaptation rather than SER benchmarking.

\section{Declaration of generative AI and AI-assisted technologies in the
manuscript preparation process}
\label{sec:ai-declaration}

This manuscript and the software that produced its results were prepared with
substantial assistance from a large language model (Anthropic Claude), used
under the direction of the author. We describe the use specifically, because a
vague declaration would misrepresent it.

\textbf{Software.} The great majority of the code in the released repository ---
the feature extraction and caching, the alignment implementations, the grid
runner and its provenance machinery, the statistical routines, the analysis
scripts, and the generators for every figure and table in this paper --- was
drafted by the model in an interactive session, against specifications, review
and correction supplied by the author at each step. The test suite was written
the same way. The author reviewed the code, directed its design, and is
responsible for it.

\textbf{Manuscript.} All sections were drafted by the model from the generated
results document, section by section, under the author's editorial direction,
and were revised by the author. Several substantive analyses in this paper exist
because the author rejected a first draft's framing and required a different
one: the bound on the among-rung differences replaced a significance claim that
could not support a null hypothesis, the one-sided and simultaneous versions of
that bound replaced a two-sided bound that answered the wrong question, the
multiple-comparison correction on the blending tests was applied at the author's
insistence, and the frame-dependence result was restated as a well-posedness
claim and a recommendation after an unverifiable claim about the literature was
removed.

\textbf{Verification.} Every numerical claim in this paper is generated from the
result files by script and cross-checked against the released results document;
no number was transcribed by hand. Every citation key resolves to a bibliography
record and the BibTeX audit reports no probable-fabrication finding. The final
author checklist retains eight proceedings or journal records for publisher-page
spot checks because they are not reliably indexed by Crossref. This point is not
routine for this project: an earlier version of this work carried fabricated
bibliography entries, produced by a model and not caught, and correcting that is
part of what this rebuild is for. Claims that cannot be supported by a verified
source will be rewritten rather than cited speculatively.

The author reviewed and edited all content and takes full responsibility for the
contents of this article.

\section*{Data availability}

Both corpora are third-party datasets and are not redistributed here. RAVDESS
\cite{livingstone2018ryerson} is publicly available under a Creative Commons
licence. CREMA-D \cite{cao2014crema} is publicly available under its own
licence from its distributors. The released code includes the manifest
construction, the exact speaker-disjoint split derivation for every seed, and
the label mapping, so the partitions used here can be reproduced exactly from
the source corpora without any data being redistributed.

All derived result files --- the 4,986-run frozen confirmatory grid, 438
retained Stage~0, Stage~1 and baseline rows, the layer sweep, the
$\varepsilon$ probe and the shift decomposition --- are released with the code,
together with the generated document listing every number the paper cites with
its interval, floor and run filter.

\section*{Ethics statement}

This work uses two existing, publicly available corpora of acted emotional
speech, collected and released by their original authors under their own ethical
approvals, and involves no new data collection and no human participants. No
attempt is made to infer the emotional state of any identifiable individual: the
speakers are performers producing scripted material, and the labels are the
intended portrayals recorded by the corpus creators rather than measurements of
felt emotion.

We note one risk specific to this application area. Emotion recognition systems
are deployed in contexts --- hiring, security screening, customer monitoring ---
where errors fall unevenly on the people being classified. The absolute
performance reported here, macro-F1 of $0.3156$ and $0.4994$ against chance
floors of $0.1665$ and $0.1656$, is far below what any such deployment would
require, and the selection failure documented in
Section~\ref{sec:results-selection} shows that a system built by an
unimpeachable protocol can land below chance without its builder being able to
tell. We report these numbers as evidence about a method, not as a capability
claim, and we would regard the cross-corpus generalisation demonstrated here as
insufficient for any consequential use.

\title{Alignment removes the wrong discrepancy:\\
       conditional shift limits covariate-shift correction\\
       in cross-corpus speech emotion recognition}

\begin{abstract}
Feature-space alignment is the standard remedy for cross-corpus speech emotion
recognition, and is usually justified by the distribution discrepancy it
removes. We run an ordered ladder of alignment methods --- standardisation,
mean shift, CORAL and multi-kernel MMD --- across 4986 runs on RAVDESS and
CREMA-D in both directions, with speaker-disjoint splits, pre-registered
comparisons and chance floors reported beside every score. Alignment is worth
$+0.12$ to $+0.20$ macro-F1 against floors near $0.166$, and no rung is shown
to beat per-dimension standardisation in either direction. Decomposing the
shift explains why: alignment drives the marginal discrepancy to $0.010\times$
its unaligned value while the conditional term falls only to $0.07\times$, so
the residual separating the corpora is $92\%$ conditional --- the component a
covariate-shift correction assumes away. Label shift is negligible (prior KL
$0.022$--$0.025$ nats) and correcting for it hurts in 238 of 240 cases while
the same estimators recover a planted shift. Three consequences follow. CORAL
at its selected regularisation provably degenerates to a mean shift, confirmed
to within $0.0061$. Source-side validation cannot detect whether alignment is
needed, separating unaligned from standardised features by $0.0018$ where the
target separates them by $0.13$, and on one seed returns a score below the
chance floor. And the discrepancy--transfer correlation reverses sign with the
measurement geometry, $-0.444$ against $+0.142$ on identical features, so the
relationship is not identified until the frame is specified. We recommend that
reported discrepancy reductions state the geometry they were measured in.
\end{abstract}

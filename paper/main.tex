\begin{abstract}
Feature-space alignment aims to reduce cross-corpus speech emotion recognition
error. We compare per-dimension standardisation, mean shift, correlation
alignment (CORAL), and multi-kernel maximum mean discrepancy (MK-MMD) across
4986 RAVDESS and CREMA-D runs in both directions. The protocol uses
speaker-disjoint splits, source-side selection, and chance floors. Alignment
adds $+0.12$ to $+0.20$ macro-F1 against floors near $0.166$. No evaluated rung
exceeds per-dimension standardisation. From unaligned to the \texttt{mkmmd\_full}
endpoint, the forward marginal diagnostic decreases to $0.010$ of its
unaligned value and the class-conditional diagnostic decreases to $0.07$,
changing their ratio from $0.14$ to $0.92$. Prior KL spans $0.022$ to $0.025$
nats. Label-shift corrections reduce macro-F1 in 238 of 240 cases while
recovering an implanted shift. CORAL converges to a scalar-rescaled mean shift
with increasing shrinkage, and its endpoint is within $0.0061$ macro-F1 of
mean shift. Forward source validation separates unaligned and standardised
features by $0.0018$, while target macro-F1 differs by $0.13$. Finally,
discrepancy-transfer correlation changes from $-0.444$ in each method's own
geometry to $+0.142$ in a fixed reference frame. Reported discrepancy
reductions must specify their measurement geometry.
\end{abstract}

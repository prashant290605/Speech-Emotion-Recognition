\begin{abstract}
Speech emotion recognition (SER) systems lose accuracy when deployed on a
corpus that differs from their training corpus, and aligning the features of
the two corpora is a common remedy. Because the deployment corpus is
unlabelled, the alignment method and its hyperparameters are usually selected
on held-out training-corpus data. This source-side validation cannot leak
target labels, but whether it is informative about the alignment choice has not
been characterised. We study this question for affine feature alignment in
cross-corpus SER. We prove that, for classifiers whose fit depends on the
features only through a stationary kernel matrix, translating the source
training and validation features by a common offset leaves every
source-validation prediction unchanged while shifting the target decision
function. We test this prediction with a read-only audit of 4,986 completed
runs between RAVDESS and CREMA-D, spanning three self-supervised speech
encoders, six alignment conditions and five classifier families. We also factor
affine alignment into relative domain transport and a common change of
classifier coordinates. In all 30 matched RBF-SVM cells per direction, source
validation returns identical scores and hyperparameters with and without
mean-shift alignment, although mean shift raises target macro-F1 by 0.1342 and
0.1969 on average. The factorisation shows that standardisation also changes
effective regularisation and kernel geometry, so comparisons of alignment
methods ordered by matched moments evaluate complete pipelines. A leakage-free
selection criterion can therefore be blind to the adaptation decision it is
used to make.
\end{abstract}

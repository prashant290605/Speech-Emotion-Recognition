\title{When marginal alignment stops helping: discrepancy geometry and model
       selection in cross-corpus speech emotion recognition}

\begin{abstract}
Feature-space alignment is an established response to cross-corpus speech emotion
recognition error. We evaluate standardisation, mean shift, covariance
alignment (CORAL), and multi-kernel maximum mean discrepancy (MK-MMD) across
4986 runs on RAVDESS and
CREMA-D in both directions, with speaker-disjoint splits, comparisons
pre-specified before the frozen confirmatory grid, and chance floors reported
beside every score. Alignment is worth
$+0.12$ to $+0.20$ macro-F1 against floors near $0.166$, and no rung is shown
to beat per-dimension standardisation in either direction. The diagnostics
document why this ordering does not track transfer: alignment drives the
marginal discrepancy to $0.010\times$ its unaligned value while the
class-conditional diagnostic falls only to $0.07\times$, raising their ratio
from $0.14$ to $0.92$. This is a residual diagnostic, not a direct estimate of
$P(y \mid x)$. The observed prior KL spans $0.022$ to $0.025$ nats, and
standard label-shift corrections hurt in 238 of 240 cases while the
same estimators recover a planted shift. Three consequences follow. As
shrinkage grows, CORAL approaches
a scalar-rescaled mean shift, confirmed to within $0.0061$. In the forward
direction, source-side validation can miss the need for alignment: it separates
unaligned from standardised features by $0.0018$ where the target separates them
by $0.13$, and on one seed returns a score below the chance floor. Finally, the
discrepancy--transfer correlation reverses sign with the measurement geometry,
  $-0.444$ against $+0.142$ on identical features, so the relationship is not
  invariant to the chosen measurement frame. We recommend that reported discrepancy
reductions state the geometry they were measured in.
\end{abstract}

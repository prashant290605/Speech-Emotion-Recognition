\begin{abstract}
Feature-space alignment aims to reduce cross-corpus speech emotion recognition
error. This controlled case study compares per-dimension standardisation, mean
shift, correlation alignment (CORAL), and affine multi-kernel maximum mean
discrepancy (MK-MMD) maps across 4986 RAVDESS and CREMA-D runs in both
directions. The protocol uses speaker-disjoint splits, source-side selection,
and chance baselines. Alignment adds $+0.12$ to $+0.20$ macro-F1 against
baselines near $0.166$. No evaluated rung is shown to outperform per-dimension
standardisation. The label-harmonisation controls retain the basic
aligned-versus-unaligned contrast after dropping RAVDESS \texttt{calm} and,
separately, after excluding \texttt{neutral} from both corpora. MMD values
depend on their basis and bandwidth rules, and the diagnostics do not identify
conditional shift. Label-shift corrections reduce macro-F1 in 238 of 240 cases
while recovering an implanted shift. CORAL converges to a scalar-rescaled mean
shift with increasing shrinkage, and its endpoint is within $0.0061$ macro-F1
of mean shift. Forward source validation separates unaligned and standardised
features by $0.0018$, while target macro-F1 differs by $0.13$. Finally, the
correlation between marginal discrepancy and target macro-F1 changes from
$-0.444$ to $+0.142$ across two stated discrepancy-measurement protocols.
These results describe two English acted-speech corpora; they do not establish
a universal alignment recommendation.
\end{abstract}
